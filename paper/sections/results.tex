\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Baseline performance}

Of the 428 complexes, 412 were prepared and docked with Vina. The 16 failures all occurred
when converting the receptor to PDBQT: in 15, Meeko could not build a residue within
\SI{8}{\angstrom} of the box from its template, and in one it could not create the receptor
at all. These
complexes are counted as failures; the other engines do not use this step and docked all
428. With the baseline protocol, Vina placed the top-ranked pose within \SI{2}{\angstrom} of
the crystal ligand for 46.3\% [41.6, 51.0] of complexes, and 41.8\% [37.2, 46.5] were also
\pbvalid{}.

The PoseBusters authors also docked these 428 complexes with Vina and released their
per-complex results with the dataset \citep{posebusters2023data}. Their run reached 52.3\%
[47.6, 57.0] top-1 success and 51.2\% \pbvalid{} success, 6.0 points above our baseline
(their run succeeded on 49 complexes where ours failed, ours on 23 where theirs failed;
exact McNemar $p = 0.003$). The two runs agreed on 356 of the 428 complexes. Our
preparation failures account for little of the difference: their run solved 5 of those 16
complexes, and on the 412 we docked the gap was 5.1 points (53.2\% against 48.1\%,
$p = 0.014$). The protocols differ in several of the choices studied here. Theirs used Vina
1.2.3 with exhaustiveness 32 and 40 poses, a cubic search box of side \SI{25}{\angstrom}
centred on the crystal ligand, and receptors protonated with Reduce and converted with
ADFR \citep{buttenschoen2024posebusters}, against exhaustiveness 8, nine poses, a box
padded by \SI{4}{\angstrom} around the ligand and PDBFixer/OpenMM preparation here. Our
\SI{2}{\angstrom}-padding arm reached 53.0\%, indistinguishable from their result
($p = 0.82$). A difference of six points between two careful Vina protocols on the same
complexes is itself comparable to the preparation effects reported below.

Search was rarely the main limitation at the baseline padding. A pose within
\SI{2}{\angstrom} was among the nine returned for 303 complexes (70.8\%), but ranked first
for only 198. Of the 230 complexes without a top-1 success, 105 were scoring failures, 89
were in the right site in the wrong binding mode, 20 were in the wrong site and 16 failed
preparation; a further 19 top poses were correct but not \pbvalid{}.

\subsection{Run-to-run variation}

Repeating the Vina baseline with four docking seeds gave top-1 success of 46.3\%, 46.7\%,
47.0\% and 47.2\% (mean 46.8\%, standard deviation 0.4 points, range 0.9 points). The
aggregate rate is therefore stable to within one point. Individual outcomes are less stable:
two seeds disagree on 17.5 complexes on average (16--19 across the six pairs). Over the
four replicates, 183 complexes always succeeded, 212 always failed and 33 (7.7\%) changed
outcome with the seed alone. We use 17.5 changed complexes as the reference level when
judging whether an arm changes more outcomes than chance.

Table~\ref{tab:comparisons} compares each arm with the Vina baseline on all 428 complexes.
Only one choice changed success beyond what multiple testing allows. A box padding of
\SI{2}{\angstrom} raised top-1 success from 46.3\% [41.6, 51.0] to 53.0\% [48.3, 57.7]
(+6.8 points; 41 complexes gained, 12 lost; Holm $p = 4.1 \times 10^{-4}$) and \pbvalid{}
success from 41.8\% to 47.9\% (+6.1 points; 41 gained, 15 lost; Holm $p = 0.003$). Larger
boxes lowered success slightly: \SI{6}{\angstrom} padding to 44.4\% ($-1.9$ points; 10
gained, 18 lost; Holm $p = 0.37$) and \SI{8}{\angstrom} to 44.2\% ($-2.1$ points; 19
gained, 28 lost; Holm $p = 0.37$).

The ligand choices moved success upwards without reaching significance after correction.
Assigning the dominant protonation state at pH 7.4 gave 49.1\% (+2.8 points; 26 gained, 14
lost; unadjusted $p = 0.08$, Holm $p = 0.24$); its \pbvalid{} effect was slightly larger
(+3.5 points; unadjusted $p = 0.04$, Holm $p = 0.14$). Regenerating the start conformer with
RDKit gave 50.7\% (+4.4 points; 52 gained, 33 lost; unadjusted $p = 0.05$, Holm
$p = 0.20$).

Every arm changed the outcome of more complexes than a change of seed did
(Table~\ref{tab:comparisons}, last column). The clearest case is the start conformer: it
changed 85 outcomes, 4.9 times the seed level, although its net effect was only +4.4
points. Most of these changes cancel, so a small net difference hides a large reshuffling of
which complexes succeed. Net success rates alone therefore understate how much a preparation
choice matters for any individual complex.

\subsection{How outcomes change: failure modes}

The tight box worked mainly by improving search. Moving from \SI{4}{\angstrom} to
\SI{2}{\angstrom} padding raised the number of complexes with any pose below
\SI{2}{\angstrom} from 303 to 338, and wrong-site top poses fell from 20 to 3. The largest
movements were wrong binding modes that became scoring failures (30 complexes, now with a
good pose that was not ranked first), scoring failures that became successes (28) and
wrong-site poses that moved into the site (12). Larger boxes did the reverse: wrong-site top
poses rose to 45 at \SI{6}{\angstrom} and 64 at \SI{8}{\angstrom}, and complexes with any
good pose fell to 282 and 278. At \SI{8}{\angstrom}, 23 wrong-mode and 19 scoring failures
became wrong-site failures. Within the same scoring function, enlarging the box lets the
search find high-scoring poses outside the true site.

The RDKit start conformer acted differently. Its gains came from scoring failures (27) and
wrong binding modes (18) that became successes, but these were offset by successes that
became scoring failures (19) or wrong modes (11), and by scoring failures that became wrong
modes (19). The start conformer thus changes which basin Vina's search ends in, in both
directions, consistent with a sampling effect rather than a systematic improvement.
Ligand protonation mainly turned wrong binding modes into scoring failures (21) and scoring
failures into successes (15); eight correct but invalid poses became \pbvalid{}.

\subsection{Preparation effects across engines}

Table~\ref{tab:engines} gives the same comparisons for Smina and Gnina. \result{rDock, once
its rerun finishes.} Smina, which reproduces Vina's scoring function, behaved like Vina
throughout. Its baseline success was 47.0\% [42.3, 51.7], and on all 428 complexes it did
not differ from Vina's (17 complexes gained, 14 lost; exact McNemar $p = 0.72$). The
\SI{2}{\angstrom} box was again the only significant arm, raising success to 54.7\%
[49.9, 59.3] (+7.7 points; 43 gained, 10 lost; Holm $p = 2.8 \times 10^{-5}$), and larger
boxes again lowered it slightly (\SI{6}{\angstrom}: $-1.4$ points; \SI{8}{\angstrom}:
$-2.8$ points; Holm $p \ge 0.38$).

Gnina, which searches with the same machinery but re-ranks poses with a convolutional
neural network, reached 62.6\% [57.9, 67.1] at baseline, 16.4 points above Vina (103
complexes gained, 33 lost; $p = 1.4 \times 10^{-9}$). The difference is not an artefact of
Vina's 16 preparation failures: on the 412 complexes both engines docked, success was
48.1\% for Vina and 63.1\% for Gnina ($p = 3.8 \times 10^{-8}$). No preparation choice
changed Gnina's success significantly. The \SI{2}{\angstrom} box gave +3.0 points and the
\SI{8}{\angstrom} box $-4.0$ points (Holm $p = 0.47$ and $0.30$); ligand protonation gave
$-3.3$ points and the RDKit conformer +0.7 points.

The mechanism behind the box effect was shared. Every engine placed more top poses in
the wrong site as the box grew: from 20, 30 and 4 complexes at \SI{4}{\angstrom} padding
(Vina, Smina, Gnina) to 64, 72 and 36 at \SI{8}{\angstrom}, and down to 3, 4 and 2 at
\SI{2}{\angstrom}. Gnina's re-scoring therefore removes most, but not all, of the
wrong-site errors that a larger box invites.

Across these three engines, the spread of top-1 success over the six preparation arms
was 8.9 points for Vina (44.2--53.0\%), 10.5 points for Smina (44.2--54.7\%) and 7.0
points for Gnina (58.6--65.7\%). The difference between engines at the baseline protocol
was 16.4 points. Choosing a re-scoring engine thus mattered about twice as much as any
single preparation choice studied here, yet the best preparation arm closed about 40\% of
the gap between Vina and Gnina (6.8 of 16.4 points).

\subsection{Which complexes are sensitive to preparation}

Part of every arm's effect falls on complexes that are unstable anyway. The 33 complexes
whose Vina outcome changed with the seed alone were strongly over-represented among each
arm's flips (odds ratios 10.5--37; Holm $p < 10^{-8}$ for every arm): with the
\SI{2}{\angstrom} box, 58\% of them flipped against 9\% of the other complexes. But they
account for a minority of most arms' flips (Table~\ref{tab:stable-flips}). The RDKit start
conformer changed the outcome of 63 complexes that never changed with the seed, the
\SI{2}{\angstrom} box 34 and protonation 24. These are effects of the preparation choice
itself, on complexes whose outcome is otherwise reproducible.

Ligand properties explained little. The clearest association was with the
\SI{2}{\angstrom} box, whose flips concentrated on larger and more flexible ligands: for
Smina, flipped complexes had a median of 10 rotatable bonds against 6 (rank-biserial
$r = 0.31$, Holm $p = 0.001$), and for Vina a median of 29 heavy atoms against 24
($r = 0.21$, Holm $p = 0.057$). For Vina the tight box mostly helped such ligands: the 41
complexes it gained had a median of 29 heavy atoms and 8 rotatable bonds, the 12 it lost
22 and 4. Protonation flips leaned towards ligands whose charge it changed, consistently
across engines (Vina: 80\% of flipped against 62\% of unflipped complexes, odds ratio
2.5, $p = 0.024$; Gnina 1.7; Smina 1.6), but no engine's association survived correction
(Holm $p \ge 0.12$). Flips from the RDKit conformer showed no association with ligand size
or flexibility in any engine, and no arm's flips were associated with the presence of a
cofactor. All but 10 of the supplied ligands are neutral, so the formal charge of the
supplied ligand itself could not be tested meaningfully.

\subsection{Interaction between protonation and box size, and a box sized to the ligand}
\label{sec:interaction}

Because each arm changes one choice from the baseline, the effects above are measured at
the baseline setting of the others. Two further sets of Vina runs tested that assumption.
First, protonation at pH 7.4 was repeated at each box padding (Table~\ref{tab:interaction}).
Its effect declined steadily as the box grew, from +4.2 points at \SI{2}{\angstrom} (30
complexes gained, 12 lost; Holm $p = 0.032$ over the four paddings) to +2.8, +1.4 and
$-0.5$ points at 4, 6 and \SI{8}{\angstrom}. Compared complex by complex with the effect at
the baseline padding, the change was not significant at any single padding (sign test on
the difference in effect, Holm $p \ge 0.46$), so the trend is consistent but not
established. It is what would be expected if a large box lets wrong-site poses dominate,
whatever the ligand's charge state, while in a tight box the charge state decides between
nearby poses. Combining the two best choices gave the best Vina protocol in this study:
\SI{2}{\angstrom} padding with protonation at pH 7.4 reached 57.2\% [52.5, 61.8], 10.9
points above the baseline (60 complexes gained, 13 lost; exact McNemar
$p = 2.3 \times 10^{-8}$) and two thirds of the way to Gnina's baseline (62.6\%).

Second, the box was sized to the ligand rather than padded around it: a cube 2.857 times
the radius of gyration of the docked conformer, centred on the crystal ligand, the
optimum \citet{feinstein2015boxsize} found for Vina. These boxes were smaller than the
baseline box for 427 complexes (median volume 0.31 times) and smaller than the
\SI{2}{\angstrom} box for 331 (0.75 times), and for 207 complexes part of the crystal
ligand lay outside the box. Success was 50.2\% [45.5, 54.9], above the baseline (+3.9
points; 46 gained, 29 lost; exact McNemar $p = 0.064$) but not above the
\SI{2}{\angstrom} box (53.0\%; $p = 0.21$). The result split cleanly on whether the
crystal ligand fitted (an exploratory, post hoc split): for the 221 complexes whose
crystal ligand lay wholly inside the box, success rose from 51.6\% to 59.7\% (22 gained,
4 lost), while for the 207 where it did not, success was unchanged (40.6\% and 40.1\%; 24
gained, 25 lost). A box scaled to the ligand works well when it contains the binding
mode, and the gain is lost where the rule truncates it; a small fixed padding around the
reference ligand avoids that failure.
