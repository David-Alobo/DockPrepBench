\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We measured how much routine preparation choices change redocking success on the
PoseBusters Benchmark set, one choice at a time and complex by complex, for four engines,
with the search space drawn from the crystal ligand.
Preparation moved success by up to 10.5 percentage points within the Vina-family engines
and by 23.8 points for rDock, less than the 33.2 points that separated the engines at the
baseline protocol, and only the size of the search box did so beyond chance; a tight box
raised success for all four engines, significantly for all but Gnina and by 11.7 points for
rDock, although on an independent set of 85 complexes only rDock's gain recurred. For Vina,
the choices also reshuffled which complexes succeeded more
than seed-to-seed variation did, even where the net rate barely moved. That sensitivity may
also be a tool: a top pose that stayed put across the preparation choices was usually
correct, and agreement across them identified correct poses far better than the docking
score for every engine, also under cross-validation and, for three of the four engines, on
the independent set, which makes re-docking with a few preparation variants a promising check
on which poses to trust, so far shown only in redocking. Docking comparisons
should therefore state the search space exactly, repeat runs over seeds and test
differences complex by complex. The open benchmark lets any new preparation step or engine
be measured against the same baseline, seed noise floor and paired statistics.
