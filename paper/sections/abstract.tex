\begin{abstract}
Docking benchmarks usually compare engines under a single, fixed preparation protocol, yet
choices such as protonation state, the starting ligand conformer and the definition of the
search space are made differently in every study. We measure how much these choices change
redocking outcomes on the PoseBusters Benchmark set (428 complexes), using paired,
one-factor-at-a-time comparisons scored by heavy-atom RMSD and PoseBusters physical
validity, for four engines: AutoDock Vina, Smina, Gnina and rDock. Within an engine,
preparation moved top-1 success by 7.0 to 10.5 percentage points across the arms for the
Vina family and by 23.8 points for rDock, against 33.2 points between the engines at the
baseline protocol. Only the size of the search box changed success beyond multiple-testing
correction, for every engine but Gnina, which it moved the same way: a \SI{2}{\angstrom} padding raised Vina
from 46.3\% [41.6, 51.0] to 53.0\% [48.3, 57.7], Smina by 7.7 points and rDock by 11.7,
while larger boxes sent top poses to wrong sites; combined with ligand protonation, it lifted
Vina to 57.2\%. Every arm changed the
outcome of more complexes than a change of docking seed did, up to 4.9 times as many, though
gains and losses largely cancelled in the net rates. Differences between engines of a few
points, measured under one protocol, are therefore within the range that preparation alone
produces; benchmarks should report the search space exactly, repeat runs over seeds and
compare protocols complex by complex. All code, configurations and per-complex results are
openly available.
\end{abstract}
